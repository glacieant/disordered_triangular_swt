\begin{abstract}

    The effect of quenched bond disorder in the triangular lattice 
    Heisenberg antiferromagnet has attracted a lot of attention lately.
    Recent experiments on crystals which are believed to realize this model
    have suggested emergence of a glassy phase when the exchange 
    interactions between the spin degrees of freedom are sufficiently 
    disordered. It is also understood that in the presence of bond disorder
    the $120^{\circ}$ non-collinear chiral order is destroyed at d = 2 lower 
    critical dimension, although, with a correlation length that scales 
    exponentially with the inverse power of the width of the bond disorder. 
    This rules out the possiblity to characterize the system without carefuly 
    eliminating the finite size effects. To this end we resort to two 
    different microscopic modelling of the disordered $J_1-J_2$ Heisenberg 
    hamiltonian, improving on the system size limitations of prior 
    calculations and also mapping out the entire phase diagram of the 
    model which corroborates to the signatures observed in experiments.

\end{abstract}

\section{The disordered $J-J'$ Heisenberg Hamiltonian on a triangular lattice}

In a disordered triangular lattice the $J-J'$ antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model
has the familiar form,
\beq\label{eq:main_ham}
    \bal
        H &= \sum_{\bra i,j \ket} J_{i,j}\vS_i\cdot\vS_j 
        + \sum_{\bra\bra i,j \ket\ket}\alpha J'_{i,j}\vS_i\cdot\vS_j    
    \eal
\eeq
with the symbols $\bra i,j\ket$ and $\bra\bra i,j\ket\ket$ representing sum over
nearest and next to nearest neighbours. This is a SU(2) symmetric hamiltonian
but the discrete translational symmetry of the lattice is lost as the couplings
$J_{i,j}$ and $J'_{i,j}$ are all uncorrelated random variables 
drawn from a random distribution $P(J,\Delta)$ with $\Delta$, the standard deviation 
characterizing the strength of the disorder. $\Delta$ is an implicit parameter for 
the disordered hamiltonian. In a clean system, however, all the couplings are fixed 
to a scale and in the absence of any external magnetic field, $\alpha$ becomes the 
sole adjustable parameter that determines the relative strength of the next to nearest 
neighbour (NNN) couplings over the nearest neighbour (NN) couplings. In the following 
we shall consider both $\alpha$ and $\Delta$ as parameters of the model and explore 
the consequent phase diagram.

For $\alpha=0$ the ground state of the homogeneous triangular lattice Heisenberg 
antiferromagnet has a non-collinear order characterized by the $120^{\circ}$ spiral
spin orientation with an ordering wave vector $\vQ=(2\pi/3,0)$. This is a well known
and well studied example of the classical N\'eel order surviving the effects of 
quantum fluctuations\cite{Jolicoeur-NN-triangular-1989,*Huse-NN-triangular-1988,
*Nishimori-NN-triangular-1985}. By tuning up $\alpha$ one can see that the classical 
ground state of this model goes through a phase transition from the three sublattice 
$120^\circ$ state to a degenerate continuum of four sublattice stripy states at 
$\alpha=1/8$\cite{Jolicoeur-NNN-triangular-1990,Kaneko-NNN-triangular-2014}. Quantum 
effects become crucial near the phase transition point and it is believed that 
spin liquid like states emerge interposed between the non-collinear $120^\circ$ and 
the \rcol{collinear} stripe phase
\cite{Hu-NNN-triangular-2015,Kaneko-NNN-triangular-2014}.

The introduction of bond disorder makes this model particularly interesting as the
non-uniformity in the coupling profile tries to compete with and unwind the effects 
of the underlying geometric frustration of the lattice and thereby makes further 
diversification of the phase map plausible. It is known that in a non-collinear 
antiferromagnet, long range N\'eel order is destroyed by random inhomogeneity in the 
exchange interactions below or at the critical dimension $d=2$
\cite{Dey-Vojta-disordered-non-collinear-2018}. Our planar
$J-J'$ heisenberg antiferromagnet on a triangular lattice for $\alpha < 1/8$ is 
precisely an example of such a system placed at the critical dimension. With the 
tentative absence of long range antiferromagnetic order in the presence of quenched 
bond disorder\cite{Watanabe-disordered-triangular-2014,
Dey-Vojta-disordered-non-collinear-2018}, the weakly frustrated 
triangular lattice antiferromagnet is amenable to a spin-glass transition
\cite{Andreanov-Chalker-frust.-dis.-magnet-2010}. In the analysis that follows, we 
show that quantum fluctuations disorders the system even further to restrict the 
surviving short range correlations of the neighbouring spins
on the lattice. The fact that quantum effects also become very important in tandem 
with disorder makes way for a possible departure from the classical degrees of 
freedom when the effects of quantum and quenched disorder is particularly strong. 
Our scheme, split between two complimentary approaches, one considering spin-wave 
fluctuations on top of the classical ground state of the disordred lattice and another 
being a slave-fermion mean field approximation capturing excitations which are 
inherently quantum in nature, explores the microscopic description of the ground state 
of this model from the onset of the breakdown of the long range antiferromagnetic order 
to the limits of very strong disorder.

\section{Spinwave excitations with finite disorder}

The spin-wave theory (SWT) for a disordered Heisenberg hamiltonian, not unlike its 
homogeneous counterpart, entails an expansion in terms of quantum excitation modes 
around the magnetically ordered classical ground state\cite{Wessel-quasicrystal-2005,
Andrade-disordered-spin-glass-2012}. The starting point for such an expansion is the
representation of the SU(2) symmetric spin operators at each lattice site with 
slave bosons (e.g. Holstein-Primakoff or Schwinger bosons) where the quantization axes 
are chosen to align with the orientation of each individual spin, as dictated by the 
classical lowest energy configuration\cite{Anderson-SWT-52,*Kubo-SWT-52}. Although the 
transformations themselves are exact, they retain the highly interacting characterstic
of the heisenberg model. The spin-wave approximation, i.e. the assumption that the 
quanutm corrections due to these bosons are weaker compared to the corresponding 
classical expectation values is an additonal input which allows a controlled expansion 
of the hamiltonian in terms of these bosonic modes. The chief distinction in the 
disordered case is that the loss of the lattice translational symmetry prevents any 
description in terms of lattice momentums, which are no longer good quantum numbers
and instead a real space formulation is necessitated.

The situation is further complicated by the non-triviality of the energy landscape of 
the disordered classical hamiltonian. In our treatment we follow a simple local 
energy minimzation scheme iteratively to reach the ground state configuration. The 
scheme involves a starting point of randomized initial spin configurations and afterwards
performing local energy minimization at each site to antialign the spins with respect to
the net local field coupled with it untill a threshold of accuracy is achieved
\cite{Walker-spin-glass-iterative-1977,Henley-spin-glass-interative-1984}. Although this
method is inherently local in nature, after a sufficient 
amount of sampling dispenses with the outliers the typical solutions do get very close to 
the true ground states in terms of the relevant small wavelength features
\cite{Henley-spin-glass-interative-1984,Sklan-spin-glass-iterative-2013}.

The classical ground state obtained through the iterative algorithm may be interpreted
as a configuration of rotation matrices $R_i$ describing the passive rotations of individual
spins with respect to the laboratory frame, $\vS_i=R_i\cdot\vS'_i$. This picture is useful 
for performing the spin-wave expansion around the quantization axis $S^{'z}_i$ attached to the 
body frame of the spins. The rotation matrices themselves can be expressed with pairs of 
Euler angles $\theta_i,\phi_i$ describing the rigid body rotation of the body axis. 
\beq\label{eq:rot_mat}
    \bal
        R_i &= 
        \begin{pmatrix}
            \cos\theta_i\cos\phi_i & -\sin\phi_i & \sin\theta_i\cos\phi_i \\
            \cos\theta_i\sin\phi_i & \cos\phi_i & \sin\theta_i\sin\phi_i \\
            -\sin\theta_i & 0 & \cos\theta_i
        \end{pmatrix}
    \eal
\eeq
For notational simplicity the hamiltonian in Eq. (\ref{eq:main_ham}) can be recast in the 
form $H = 1/2\sum_{i,j}J_{i,j}\vS_i\cdot\vS_j$, where both the parameters $\alpha$ and $\Delta$ 
are implicit and they control the magnitudes of the couplings $J_{i,j}$s which are otherwise 
zero unless $(i,j) \in \text{NN or NNN}$ lattice points. In the rotated frame the hamiltonian 
reads, 
\beq\label{eq:rot_ham}
    \bal
        H &= \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}J_{i,j}(R^{-1}_i\cdot R_j)_{\alpha\beta}
        S^{'\alpha}_i S^{'\beta}_j
    \eal
\eeq
with the standard Holstein-Primakoff (HP) bosons introduced in a very assymetric way,
\beq\label{eq:hol_prima}
    \bal
        S^{'z}_i &= S-a^\dagger_i a_i \\
        S^{'+}_i &= \sqrt{2S - a^\dagger_i a_i} \ a_i \\
        S^{'-}_i &= a^\dagger_i \ \sqrt{2S - a^\dagger_i a_i}    
    \eal
\eeq
. In a disordered lattice of a finite size N, one has to consider all these N bosonic 
operators, $a_i$s, together while performing the spin-wave approximation of the 
hamilonian (\ref{eq:rot_ham}) in real space. The consideration of a 2N dimensional 
vector $\vec{\gamma}=(\vec{a},\vec{a}^\dagger)$ consisting of the HP bosons and 
their hermitian conjugates offers a relative simplification of the resulting expression. 
The expansion, as obtained for the Heisenberg model on a generic lattice upto third order
\cite{Wollny-phd-thesis-2016}, can be neatly collected in the powers of the $\gamma_i$ 
operators.
\beq\label{eq:sw_ham}
    \bal
        H &= H^0
        + \sum_i^{2N} K^{(1)}_i\gamma_i
        + \sum_{i,j}^{2N} K^{(2)}_{i,j}\gamma_i\gamma_j \\
        &+ \sum_{i,j,k}^{2N} K^{(3)}_{i,j,k}\gamma_i\gamma_j\gamma_k
        + \Ord(\gamma^4) 
    \eal
\eeq
with the coefficients given by,
\begin{widetext}
    \beq\label{eq:sw_coeff}
        \bal
            H_0 &= \frac{S^2}{2}\sum^N_{i,j}J_{i,j}
            \Big[\cos\theta_i\cos\theta_j
            +\cos(\phi_i-\phi_j)\sin\theta_i\sin\theta_j
            \Big] \\
            K^{(1)}_i &= \begin{cases} 
                            \Gamma^{(1)}_i=
                            \frac{S^{3/2}}{\sqrt{2}}
                            \sum^N_j J_{i,j}\Big[ 
                            \cos(\phi_i-\phi_j)
                            \cos\theta_i\sin\theta_j
                            -\sin\theta_i\cos\theta_j
                            -\text{i}
                            \sin(\phi_i-\phi_j)\sin\theta_j
                            \Big], \ \text{ if } i \leq N \\
                            
                            \Gamma^{(1)*}_{i-N}, \ \text{ if } i > N 
                        \end{cases}

            K^{(2)}_i &= \begin{cases} 
                            \frac{S^{3/2}}{\sqrt{2}}
                            \sum^N_j J_{i,j}\Big[ 
                            \cos(\phi_i-\phi_j)
                            \cos\theta_i\sin\theta_j
                            -\sin\theta_i\cos\theta_j
                            -\text{i}
                            \sin(\phi_i-\phi_j)\sin\theta_j
                            \Big], \ \text{ if } i \leq N \\
                            
                            \frac{S^{3/2}}{\sqrt{2}}
                            \sum^N_j J_{i,j}\Big[ 
                            \cos(\phi_i-\phi_j)
                            \cos\theta_i\sin\theta_j
                            -\sin\theta_i\cos\theta_j
                            -\text{i}
                            \sin(\phi_i-\phi_j)\sin\theta_j
                            \Big], \ \text{ if } i \leq N 
                        \end{cases}

        \eal
    \eeq
\end{widetext}
